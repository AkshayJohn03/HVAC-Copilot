HVAC-Copilot whiteboard lecture — narration script (scene-delimited)
Voice: Kokoro af_heart. Numbers written for TTS; exact figures appear on screen.

=== SCENE 1 — The rooftop problem ===
Picture a technician standing on a rooftop. A heat pump is flashing fault code E oh four. The repair manual is a three hundred page PDF sitting in the van. A web search returns forum guesses — for equipment that can electrocute you, or vent refrigerant into the sky. This is the problem HVAC-Copilot was built for: a repair manual that answers back. You type a question, and the manual's own words come back — quoted, with the section they came from. In the next few minutes, we'll walk through how it works, piece by piece.

=== SCENE 2 — The core idea: an open-book exam ===
Here's the core idea. The assistant never answers from memory. It takes an open-book exam. First, the manuals are read once, and stored in a searchable index. When a question arrives, the system finds the few passages that matter, hands only those passages to the language model — the L L M — and asks it to answer strictly from what's on the desk. This pattern is called retrieval augmented generation, or rag. The full set of documents is called the corpus. And the pipeline has five stations: parse, chunk, index, retrieve, compose. Everything else in this lecture is just those five stations, looked at closely.

=== SCENE 3 — Chunking ===
Station two is chunking — and it's where most of the quality is won. A chunk is one meaningful piece of the manual: small enough to retrieve precisely, big enough to make sense on its own. This repo doesn't slice blindly every few hundred characters. It walks the manual's heading tree — unit, chapter, section, step — and cuts where a human engineer would. That's section-aware hierarchical chunking. One rule matters most: tables are never split. A fault table row torn from its header is meaningless — so every table stays whole, as a single chunk. Every chunk also carries metadata: which manual, which unit model, whether it's a safety section. Fifteen chapters become one hundred and eleven chunks.

=== SCENE 4 — Two librarians ===
Now the question comes in, and two librarians read it in parallel. The first is B M twenty five — a keyword matcher. It scores chunks by how often your exact words appear, weighted by how rare those words are across the corpus. Fault codes are exact strings, so this librarian nails them. The second is dense retrieval — it matches meaning. Every chunk was converted into an embedding: a vector, a list of numbers representing what the text means. Your question becomes a vector too, and similarity is the angle between them — cosine similarity. So "not cooling" lands near "low cooling capacity", even though not one word matches. Neither librarian is enough alone. So the two ranked lists are merged by reciprocal rank fusion — R R F — where a chunk scores for every list it appears in. That's hybrid retrieval: keyword for precision, meaning for slang, fused.

=== SCENE 5 — The fault-code fast path ===
But before any of that runs, there's a shortcut. A regular expression scans the question for a fault code. If that code exists in the index, the system skips search entirely, and renders the manual's own table row — verbatim. Code E oh four. Display: I C E. Symptom: evaporator coil iced. Likely cause, corrective action, severity. The A I never rewords a fault table. A code hit is a lookup, not a generation — fast, deterministic, and quotable.

=== SCENE 6 — Citations ===
Every answer carries receipts. Citations name the document, the chapter, the section — down to the full breadcrumb path. And they're derived from what the answer actually used, so they can be measured, not just trusted. A groundedness score checks the answer's words and numbers against the retrieved passages — every number in the answer must exist in the manual. If the passages don't contain the answer, the system says what's missing, instead of guessing.

=== SCENE 7 — The safety refusal ===
Now the part I care about most. Some questions are safety-critical: refrigerant handling, pressurized systems, live electrical work. Watch two questions side by side. "How much refrigerant does the unit carry?" That's a spec lookup. Naming a factory charge weight synthesizes nothing — so the copilot answers from the spec table. But "recover the refrigerant"? Same noun — now a work verb. That's hazardous work. And if no section tagged as safety documentation supports an answer, the assistant refuses before the language model is even called. Quote: "I can't give procedural guidance for this. Stop and contact a certified H V A C technician." It names the topic, and cites the nearest safety section by title. The refusal is computed from retrieval state — not from a model's opinion — so it's deterministic, testable, and auditable. And the exemption is deliberate: a guardrail that fires on harmless questions trains people to ignore it. That's alarm fatigue — and it's why the detector has two tiers.

=== SCENE 8 — The numbers ===
So — does it work? Measured, not claimed. Thirty-eight golden questions — a fixed exam, written in advance — score recall at five of one point zero. For every single question, the right section appeared in the top five retrieved chunks. Zero safety violations across the whole set — the refusal behavior is not an accident. Citation precision measures zero point seven four. That's the honest weak spot — the overview section sometimes outranks the specific one, and a cross-encoder reranker is the planned fix. Groundedness: zero point eight one. And re-ingesting an unchanged corpus re-embeds nothing — content hashing means you only pay for what changed.

=== SCENE 9 — Recap ===
Thirty-second recap. Manuals are parsed into meaningful chunks — tables kept whole. Questions are understood: slang translated, fault codes extracted. Search runs two ways — keyword and meaning — fused into one list. A fault code never reaches the A I; it's an exact lookup. Answers cite their sections, with numbers anchored to the manual. And safety-critical work gets a refusal grounded in the index, with a human escalation. That's HVAC-Copilot: a repair manual that answers back — and knows when not to.
